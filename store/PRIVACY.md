# Privacy Policy — Bulk Clock

**Last updated: 27 August 2026**

Bulk Clock is a nutrition and training tracker for Android. This policy explains
what the app does with your information. The short version is that it does very
little, because almost nothing leaves your phone.

## What we collect

**Nothing.** There is no account, no sign-up, no analytics SDK, no advertising
identifier and no crash-reporting service. The developer has no server that
receives your data and no way to see what you log.

## What the app stores, and where

Everything you enter is written to your device's own private app storage:

- Your profile — name, age, sex, height, weight, goals and targets
- Everything you log — meals, foods, portions, water, supplements
- Your training programme and every set you record
- Weight, body measurements and progress photos
- Settings, reminders and check-in answers

This data stays in the app's private directory. Other apps cannot read it.
Uninstalling Bulk Clock deletes all of it permanently.

**Photos** you attach to meals, your profile or your progress log are copied into
the app's private storage at a reduced size. They are never uploaded anywhere.

## When the app uses the internet

Bulk Clock works fully offline. It makes a network request in exactly three
situations, all of them optional and all of them started by you:

1. **Barcode scanning.** The barcode number you scan is sent to
   [Open Food Facts](https://world.openfoodfacts.org) to look up the product.
   Only the barcode is sent — no identifier, no other data.
2. **Food photos.** The app asks Wikipedia for the lead image of a food to
   illustrate the database. Only the article title is sent.
3. Nothing else. There are no other outbound requests.

Both services will see the network request itself, which necessarily includes
your IP address, as any web request does. Their own policies apply to that:
[Open Food Facts](https://world.openfoodfacts.org/privacy) and the
[Wikimedia Foundation](https://foundation.wikimedia.org/wiki/Policy:Privacy_policy).

If you never scan a barcode and turn off food photos, the app makes no network
requests at all.

## Permissions, and why

| Permission | What it is for |
| --- | --- |
| Camera | Scanning barcodes, and taking meal or progress photos |
| Photos / media | Choosing an existing photo from your gallery |
| Notifications | Meal, training, supplement and check-in reminders |
| Exact alarms | Making those reminders arrive at the minute rather than whenever the system feels like it |
| Full-screen intent | Showing a reminder over the lock screen, the way a calendar alert does |
| Ignore battery optimisation | Asking Android not to defer your reminders while the phone is idle |

Every one of these can be refused. The app keeps working without them; you lose
the specific feature, nothing else.

## Sharing

Your data is never sold, shared, or transmitted to the developer or to any third
party. The only way data leaves your device is when **you** export it — the
export writes a file and hands it to Android's share sheet, and where it goes
from there is your choice.

## Children

Bulk Clock is not designed for children under 13 and should not be used to plan
the diet of a child. Calorie and macronutrient targets for growing children are
a matter for a doctor or a registered dietitian, not an app.

## Your rights

Because the data never leaves your device, there is nothing for the developer to
disclose, correct or delete on your behalf. You have direct control:

- **Export** — Settings → Your data → Export, as JSON or CSV
- **Delete** — Settings → Reset app, or uninstall

## Health disclaimer

Bulk Clock provides general nutrition and training information. It is not
medical advice, and it is not a substitute for a doctor or a dietitian. Calorie
targets are estimates from a population formula. If you have a medical
condition, are pregnant, or have a history of disordered eating, speak to a
professional before following anything the app suggests.

## Changes

If this policy changes, the updated version will be published here and the "last
updated" date changed. Because the app collects nothing, a change would most
likely mean a new optional online feature — which would be opt-in.
